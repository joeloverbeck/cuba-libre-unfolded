## Every choice within this task

**Terror locations and order.** Only Santiago and Oriente initially contain an M26 Underground Guerrilla. Own removal can delete these options; nothing in this task can create another location. The target sets are none, Santiago, Oriente, or both, with either order for both, subject to the resources and pieces present at execution. The zero-space Op Only reading is explained below; it cannot accompany Kidnap. Each selected space costs 1 Resource, activates one of that space's Underground M26 Guerrillas, adds one Terror marker and shifts its level one step toward Active Opposition. Each space can be selected only once (R 3.1/3.3.4). Santiago becomes Passive Opposition; Oriente becomes Active Opposition. Where multiple indistinguishable Underground pieces exist the player may identify the piece, but routine activation/counting is automatic.

**Direction reconciliation.** R 3.3.4's “OR, if 26July” is a faction-specific replacement for the ordinary neutralizing shift. The M26 third-printing aid explicitly gives the Active Opposition direction, while the DR/Syndicate aids give Neutral; P's tutorial p.4 applies the former. Do not invent a second M26 choice to neutralize Oriente. Terror does not remove Police, move pieces, change their faction or change Control merely by activating a Guerrilla.

**Kidnap.** Choose none, or once in Santiago, targeting Government only. Oriente is neither a City, EC nor open-Casino space; every EC lacks a Guerrilla; no other Terror location can appear. Santiago has `2 M26 Guerrillas > 1 Police`, no Casino and no Cash. Both active and Underground Guerrillas count for outnumbering (R 1.4.3/4.3.3). Kidnap itself has no Resource cost and does not activate a Guerrilla. It rolls one six-sided die and transfers up to that result from Government to M26. There is no Casino to close, Cash ransom, Aid change, reroll or Raúl effect.

Kidnap can occur immediately before, during or immediately after this Operation, provided Santiago was or will be a Terror space (R 4.1/4.3.3). This includes after Terror has activated one Santiago Guerrilla: two total Guerrillas still outnumber one Police. It includes after both Terror spaces have resolved, before final completion. If a Guerrilla was removed, recalculate outnumbering at Kidnap time. Infiltrate accompanies Rally/March; Ambush accompanies Attack; neither is an alternative accompanying this Terror (R 4.3.1–2). Kidnap may not be repeated or split across times/spaces.

**Funding and completion.** Initially the only self-funded route to a nonempty Terror is Kidnap in Santiago before its Terror; Government's 6 Resources guarantees at least 1 on every die result. Transfers can instead fund either Terror first and can make Kidnap unnecessary. Offer remaining legal Terror, unused legal Kidnap, transfers, own removal, and Finish whenever no accompanying Terror is owed. The player need not spend every available Resource or use the Special Activity. Finalizing without Kidnap records Op Only; finalizing with Kidnap records Op + Special Activity. There is no Cash to Launder, even on the no-Kidnap branch.

**Transfers and decision owners.** While M26 executes, R 1.5.1/1.8 permit Resource transfers in either direction between M26 and each other faction, regardless of eligibility. Government↔DR, Government↔Syndicate and DR↔Syndicate are not legal in this window because neither endpoint is executing. The **giver** chooses the quantity and authorizes the transfer. M26 cannot take a promised donation; an offer/refusal/nonbinding agreement is not a Resource change. Local human controls must support those other-faction decisions without built-in bots or external faction-agent infrastructure. A donor can decline, leaving M26 to use an available alternative or wait. No forced donation is assumed in an expectation.

At each transfer allow whole-number `n` with `0 <= n <= giver resources` and resulting recipient Resources at most 49; `n=0` is deliberate non-transfer. Subtract `n` from giver and add `n` to receiver atomically. The position has only 26 total Resources and no income operation, so the 49 cap cannot actually bind in any reachable continuation. Repeated transfers remain possible, not a fixed once-per-operation allowance. Expose a bounded development-seat session and a save/quit path rather than inventing a game-rule time limit. Four distinct faction seats retain four-faction rules; convenient human testing of their decisions must not silently enable the separate fewer-player variant.

**Voluntary removal.** R 1.4.1 permits the *executing* player faction to remove any of its own pieces to Available Forces. Here that is M26 only: either/both Santiago Guerrillas, the Oriente Guerrilla and the Sierra Maestra Base. This permission includes the Base and active Guerrillas after Terror. Government cannot remove its Police merely because it is the Kidnap target; the other nonexecuting factions cannot close Casinos or remove their pieces in this window. Removing a Guerrilla neither relocates nor flips it into a new on-map Underground piece. No Rally replacement occurs.

**Commitment policy proposed for acceptance.** The sources require Kidnap's accompanying Terror and permit flexible timing, but do not define software transactions for a player who tries to destroy that future obligation. Preserve all feasible orderings; do not solve this by disabling transfers or own removal. A Kidnap before Santiago's Terror records an unresolved obligation to complete Terror there. Keep one Santiago Underground Guerrilla and its 1-Resource payment feasible until that obligation resolves. With the initial position, taking the Kidnap roll is safe for all six results. After the roll, refuse a proposed transfer/removal only if it would make the required Terror impossible; allow it later after that Terror. Before the roll, evaluate feasibility without assuming a donor will give money or a favorable die result. If Government has been depleted, M26 must already have the necessary payment or receive an actual transfer before committing such a Kidnap.

Do not apply that guard before an accompanying Terror is actually owed. Removing one Santiago Guerrilla at zero Resources is allowed: Kidnap becomes unavailable, but an actual later donation can still fund Terror. Removing all Underground Guerrillas is also a voluntary choice. The best-supported reading for deliberate completion with no selected Terror spaces is **zero-space Op Only**: R 3.1/3.3.4 set no minimum number of spaces, whereas R 4.1 explicitly requires at least one for a Special Activity. Under this reading, Finish with no resolved spaces and no Kidnap consumes the already-selected Operation, gives no Pass income and allows the next faction only Pass/LimOp. This includes a player who deliberately removed all their eligible pieces. It does not allow Kidnap without its required Santiago Terror. Thus all own-removal and donation choices remain available before that obligation, including states that can only wait or finish without Terror.

Before the first effect, inspection/cancellation returns to the same prepared task without changing game state. Once a roll or effect is committed it cannot be cancelled into a free Kidnap, rerolled, converted into a Pass or undone by restarting. Save/quit preserves the pending task. After fulfillment of any Kidnap obligation, Finish is always possible, even after removing every remaining M26 piece or giving away every remaining Resource.

These are explicit source interpretations and a proposed local resolution policy inferred from R 3.1/3.3.4 and 4.1/4.3.3, **not an official publisher ruling about adversarial micro-timing or zero-space Operations**. They belong in the combined source review, not separate requests to approve internal transactions. A removal that destroys an already-owed Terror can instead occur after fulfilling it. Recommend this reading for the bounded slice, with its narrower fidelity claim; an insistence on publisher-certified interruption/zero-space semantics would require separate clarification before that stronger claim. Do not label prefix refusal itself independently source-proven.
